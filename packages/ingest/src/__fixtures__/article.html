How sleep shapes memory

Sleep is not a pause for the brain. During deep sleep, the hippocampus replays the events of the day, and the cortex slowly stores them for the long term. Researchers call this process consolidation, and it explains why a night of sleep after studying helps recall.

Two kinds of sleep

Slow-wave sleep seems to help facts and events, while REM sleep seems to help skills and emotional memories. Most nights cycle through both kinds four or five times, so cutting sleep short removes the later cycles that are rich in REM sleep.

  • Keep a regular bedtime so the cycles stay complete and predictable over the week.
  • Review hard material in the evening, then sleep on it before testing yourself again.

We remember better after we sleep, not only because we rested, but because the brain was busy.

In short, sleep is part of learning. Treat it as a study session that you do not have to plan, and protect it before exams rather than trading it for more hours of review.
